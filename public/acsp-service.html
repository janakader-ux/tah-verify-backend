Authorised Corporate Service Provider identity verification

Tax And Accounting Hub Ltd is authorised by Companies House to verify the identity of directors, PSCs, LLP members and general partners on their behalf — properly, and to the required legal standard.

The legal basis

What is an ACSP, and why does it exist?

An Authorised Corporate Service Provider (ACSP) is a firm — typically an accountant, solicitor or company formation agent — that Companies House has authorised to verify individuals' identities on its behalf. The requirement was introduced by the Economic Crime and Corporate Transparency Act 2023, alongside the Registrar's (Identity Verification by ACSPs) Rules 2025, which together brought mandatory identity verification to the UK company register for the first time.

Checks performed by an ACSP must meet the Companies House identity verification standard. According to GOV.UK's own guidance on meeting the Companies House identity verification standard, six core steps apply to every check: asking for identity information, gathering evidence of identity, checking that evidence is genuine, checking the identity belongs to the person presenting it, keeping records for a defined period, and deciding whether to proceed with submission.

As your ACSP, we complete all six steps on your behalf and submit an Identity Verification Statement directly to Companies House — you never have to navigate the process alone.

Who this applies to

Who must verify their identity

  • Directors

    Every director of a UK company — UK-resident or overseas.

  • People with significant control

    Anyone registered as a PSC of a company.

  • LLP members

    Designated and non-designated members of an LLP.

  • General partners

    General partners of a limited partnership.

  • Persons filing on behalf of a company

    Anyone who files documents with Companies House on a company's behalf.

Two verification routes

Digital or in-person — both fully permitted

GOV.UK's guidance is explicit that identity checks can be completed remotely, in person, or through an ACSP using a commercial identity service provider. We offer both routes honestly, and recommend whichever suits your documents.

Online / Digital (IDVT)

A secure link to your phone. Photograph your document, take a live selfie, done in about 10 minutes. Verified through our sub-processor, TrustID, a certified Identity Service Provider.

Accepted — any ONE of:

  • EU / Norway / Iceland / Liechtenstein biometric national identity card

Process: secure link sent to you → smartphone photo of document + live selfie (~10 minutes) → reviewed by us → submitted to Companies House.

Route B · No biometric passport needed

In-Person / Manual

Two original documents, examined in person by a trained reviewer at our office. No photocopies or photographs of copies.

Either 2 from Group A, or 1 from Group A + 1 from Group B:

Group A — photo ID

  • Non-biometric passport
  • EEA national identity card
  • UK or international photocard driving licence
  • UK biometric residence permit (BRP) or biometric residence card (BRC)

Your Companies House personal code, explained

Once your identity has been verified — whether through GOV.UK One Login or through us as your ACSP — Companies House issues an 11-character personal code. This code is personal to you, not to any company you direct or hold significant control over (GOV.UK). You verify your identity once, and the same code is then reused to confirm your verified status for every appointment you hold — every directorship, every PSC role, across every company — rather than repeating the identity check for each one. You do still need to actively provide that code for each role and each company as your filings require it: it links to each appointment individually, it does not link your appointments together automatically. Keep it secure, in the same way you'd protect an HMRC Unique Taxpayer Reference — and if you ever believe it's been compromised, Companies House can cancel it and issue a new one.

No overseas exemption — but a clear route

Overseas and non-UK-resident directors

There is no exemption from Companies House identity verification for directors who live outside the UK. The Economic Crime and Corporate Transparency Act 2023 applies the same duty regardless of nationality or residence. What changes for overseas directors is which route actually works in practice.

GOV.UK's self-verification service, One Login, accepts a biometric passport from any country — genuinely useful if you hold one. But its other accepted documents (UK driving licence, UK BRP, UK BRC, UK Frontier Worker permit) are all UK-specific, and its two fallback options for people without those documents are explicitly offered "if you live in the UK," per GOV.UK. That leaves a real gap for non-UK residents without a biometric passport — no UK phone number, no UK bank or credit footprint, and no UK address to fall back on.

As an ACSP, we close that gap. GOV.UK confirms that identity verification through an authorised agent "can be done from any country" (GOV.UK), using a wider document list — including foreign driving licences, national ID cards, visas and work permits — with the one added rule that at least one document must be government-issued if you're not UK-resident. Verification can be completed by secure digital link at a time that fits your time zone, and your foreign home address is accepted and never published on the public register. If you do not hold a biometric passport, the two-document route requires an in-person appointment at our Bedford or London–Stratford office, at £125 per applicant. Overseas remote verification is £119, all-inclusive. UK remote verification is £49; standard office appointments are £125 for either residency group.

Working with firms and groups

For accountants, law firms and corporate groups

If you're managing identity verification across a client base, a company secretarial function, or a corporate group with several subsidiaries, the challenge isn't the individual check — it's coordinating dozens of confirmation statement dates, PSC 14-day windows and document types without anything slipping through. Each person's identity still has to be verified individually; GOV.UK confirms the Companies House personal code is "personal to you, not your company," so there is no single check that covers a group.

What we offer instead is a consistent process at volume: one named point of contact managing your batch, the same compliance checklist applied to every individual, a shared view of who's completed their check and who's still pending, and seven-year record retention applied consistently across your whole client base or group — so there's one audit trail to point to, not a patchwork of informal notes. Firms that would rather not register as their own Companies House authorised agent can refer verification work to us under a straightforward working relationship, while remaining their client's primary point of contact throughout.

Frequently asked questions

Everything else you might want to know

Is this fully remote?

Yes — no travel or appointment is needed for the digital route. The two-document route is different: original documents have to be examined in person, by appointment at our Bedford or London–Stratford office.

Can someone else do the camera steps for me?

No — you must complete the identity check personally. It cannot be completed on your behalf by a third party.

Can I post my documents to you?

No — please never post original identity documents to us. The two-document route is carried out in person: you bring your originals to your appointment at Bedford or London–Stratford, a trained reviewer examines them while you wait, and you take them away with you the same day. Nothing is retained and nothing travels by post.

My passport has expired — can I still use it?

Sometimes. Digital checks tolerate passports expired by up to 6 months; in-person checks can tolerate up to 18 months. Please check with us first.

I've changed my name — what do I need?

A marriage certificate, civil partnership certificate, or deed poll showing the name change.

Is my personal code still valid if my document later expires?

Yes — once issued, your personal code remains valid indefinitely, even if the document you verified with later expires.

What if my digital check fails for a technical reason?

We'll re-issue your verification link once, free of charge.

What does it cost?

£49 for UK remote verification; £119 for overseas remote verification; £125 for a standard office appointment for either residency group. All-inclusive.

What happens once I'm verified?

Once you connect your personal code to your Companies House record, the public register shows your name, your ACSP (Tax And Accounting Hub Ltd), our AML supervisory body (AAT), and the date — never your documents or home address.
