Local or remote

Does a local ACSP actually help?

People search for a verification agent near them for an understandable reason: this is an identity check, and identity checks feel like something that should happen face to face. In practice the location of your ACSP is a matter of comfort rather than compliance. GOV.UK requires that registered ACSPs must be based in the UK and be supervised by a UK anti-money-laundering supervisory body, but it does not require them to be near you, and the digital identity check is completed on your own phone wherever you happen to be.

Where location genuinely matters is the two-document route, which involves original documents being examined. We hold those appointments at our Bedford and London–Stratford offices, and they are the only places we can examine original documents, so anyone taking that route has to travel to one of them. That is why our city pages say plainly whether an office visit is realistic from that city rather than implying a national branch network that does not exist.

The free alternatives are worth knowing about too. GOV.UK One Login is free and direct (GOV.UK), and UK residents can verify at a Post Office after receiving the confirmation email, a route that takes about 15 days. Of all verifications completed to 31 March 2026, 3 million went through One Login and 783,000 through ACSPs (Annual Report and Accounts 2025–26). We are the paid, managed option, and we would rather you chose it knowing the free ones exist.

The requirement

Who must verify, and by when

The duty is national. Whichever city page you arrived from, the rules below are the ones that apply to you.

GOV.UK lists the people who must verify as a director, the equivalent of a director, which includes members, general partners and managing officers, a director of an overseas company registered in the UK, a person with significant control, and anyone registering as an Authorised Corporate Service Provider. People who file on behalf of a company, limited partnerships, corporate directors, corporate LLP members and officers of corporate PSCs will be required to verify at a later date.

Identity verification became a legal requirement on 18 November 2025. Companies House is explicit that this date is not a deadline in itself; it marks the start of a 12-month transition period that ends in November 2026, by the end of which Companies House expects to have verified 6 to 7 million individuals (Annual Report and Accounts 2025–26). Your own deadline sits inside that window and depends on your role:

  • Existing directors: by the company's next confirmation statement date.
  • New directors: before appointment, and at incorporation for a new company.
  • Directors of overseas companies registered in the UK: by the anniversary of the UK establishment's registration.
  • A PSC who is also a director: within the 14-day period starting the day after the confirmation statement date.
  • A PSC who is not a director: within the first 14 days of their birth month.

Once you have verified, Companies House issues an 11 character personal code. It is personal to you and not to your company, and if you are a director of multiple companies you only need to verify once and then use the same code for every appointment.

If you do not verify

What happens if the deadline passes

These consequences apply identically in every part of the UK.

Companies House states that it is unlawful for a director to act as a director without completing identity verification, and that the offence arises under section 167M of the Companies Act. Three enforcement routes are available: criminal prosecution, referral to the Insolvency Service, and financial penalties. Prosecution may lead to criminal conviction, director disqualification and fines, and non-compliance can be annotated on the public register for anyone to see.

There is also an immediate, practical consequence that arrives long before any enforcement action. All directors need to verify before the company's next confirmation statement is filed, otherwise the filing will be rejected. The campaign guidance puts it more bluntly still: without verification you will not be able to make any filings for your company or start a new company. For most people that rejected filing, not the prospect of prosecution, is what makes this urgent.

Questions

UK location questions

What directors ask us about location, offices and coverage.

Does it matter where in the UK my company is registered?

No. Identity verification is a Companies Act requirement and the standard is the same across England and Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland. The England and Wales register held 5,114,182 companies at 31 March 2026, Scotland 275,310 and Northern Ireland 89,553.

Do I have to use an ACSP that is near me?

No. Registered ACSPs must be based in the UK, but there is no requirement for your ACSP to be in your city. The digital identity check is completed on your own phone wherever you are.

Which cities do you have offices in?

Bedford and London–Stratford. Applicants elsewhere in the UK who need the two-document route do have to travel to one of those two offices; the digital route needs no appointment at all.

Which UK city has the most registered companies?

London, with 1,109,035 live companies, followed by Manchester with 112,013 and Birmingham with 98,741, on the Companies House bulk data snapshot of 1 August 2026.

Is there a free way to do this?

Yes. GOV.UK One Login is free and direct, and UK residents can also use the Post Office route after receiving the confirmation email, which takes about 15 days. An ACSP is the paid, managed alternative.
